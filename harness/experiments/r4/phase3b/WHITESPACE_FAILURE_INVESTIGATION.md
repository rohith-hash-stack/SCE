# Phase 3b: the line-181 whitespace failure (offline investigation, 2026-11-28)

**What this is:** a post-hoc investigation of the recurring refused edit at `tests/conftest.py:181` on p3-hidden-15.
- It is not a pre-registered analysis, and makes no new experimental claim.
- No code, prompt, task, score file, transcript or analysis output was changed, and nothing was re-run.

**Inputs:**
- the verified ledger (`verification/audit_ledger.json`), which gives each run's id and shard;
- the 60 p3-hidden-15 turn logs at `reports/r4/phase3b_shard<k>/<run_id>.jsonl`;
- the litestar source at the task commit;
- the tool implementations: `harness/experiments/r3/shared_tools.py`, `harness/experiments/r3/tools.py` and
  `harness/experiments/r3/mistral_raw.py`.
- The answer key and the hidden tests were not read.

**Script:** `harness/experiments/r4/phase3b_ws181.py`, new and separate from the experiment's analysis.

**Outputs**, in `verification/`:
- `ws181_attempts.csv`: 1,093 attempts, one row each;
- `ws181_summary.json`: per-arm counts, per-run sequences, origin classification.

**Labels:**
- **[VERIFIED]:** read directly from the transcripts, the source or the code.
- **[HYPOTHESIS]:** consistent with the evidence, not established by it.
- **[UNKNOWN]:** the evidence does not say.

## 1. Executive summary

**The line [VERIFIED]:**
- `tests/conftest.py:181` is `) -> SessionMiddleware[ClientSideSessionBackend]:`, with no leading whitespace.
- It closes a multi-line signature between two lines indented by four spaces.
- The file contains no tab character.

**What the PRISM arms did [VERIFIED]:** they edited the place on its own, as one line, and added whitespace the line
does not have.
- **First attempts:** in 40 of the 45 A, C and E runs, the first attempt carried **four spaces**. In the other 5 (all
  E) it had none.
- **Later attempts:** many switched to **one leading tab**. A: 64 of 507 attempts; C: 105 of 183; E: 304 of 383.
- **The loop:** the model repeated refused calls until the turn cap. 976 of the 1,073 A/C/E attempts were exact
  repeats of an earlier call.

**Where the whitespace came from:**
- **Every attempt that first introduced a tab** (15 of them) came after line 181 had been shown in the numbered
  `N<TAB>text` layout [VERIFIED]. Of these:
  - 9 came on the turn right after the edit tool's own error listing `181<TAB>) -> …`;
  - 4 came right after a `read_file`/`prism_read` listing;
  - 2 came after a loop refusal, with a `prism_read` listing just before.
- **The tab is therefore most plausibly the listing's separator, copied** [HYPOTHESIS]. Its timing supports this. It
  cannot be proven, and which listing, error or read, cannot be separated, because both had always been shown.
- **The four spaces** match the neighbouring lines and, in 50 of 54 new four-space variants, the indentation of the
  model's previous applied edit [HYPOTHESIS: copied from neighbouring lines, or generated by the model; not
  separable].

**Recovery [VERIFIED]:**
- **No run ever corrected the single line's whitespace on its own.** The only accepted one-line edits of line 181 in
  all 80 runs are C's 10, each made after C's note gave the exact text.
- **Every other accepted edit of line 181 was a multi-line edit anchored on a neighbouring line** (179 or 180): the
  baseline's 12, A's 2, E's 6 and C's other 1.
- **The baseline never had a whitespace mismatch at line 181:** every one of its attempts started at a line with
  correct leading whitespace.

**The reported recovery findings reproduce [VERIFIED]:**
- C recovered at line 181 within 5 turns in 7 of the 12 runs with a trigger there;
- A recovered in 0 of its 14;
- all 43 of C's recoveries on p3-hidden-15 followed a note, and 42 used its exact text.

## 4. `read_file` investigation

**The implementation** [VERIFIED] (`SharedTools._read_file`, `shared_tools.py`):
- each line is shown as `f"{i}\t{line}"`, a number, one **tab**, then the source text exactly as in the file;
- the header is `tests/conftest.py (lines a-b of N)`;
- `prism_read` uses the same layout (`tools.py`, `_numbered`).

**Why it is a tab:** the code's own comment explains the choice, a recorded decision of 2026-11-06:

> "The number and the line are separated by one tab: indentation is spaces, so the prefix cannot be read as part of it
> (the qwen notice check, 2026-11-06: a space gutter was copied into `edit_file`'s `old` text)."

So the tab is a display separator only. It is not part of the source content.

**How the model receives it** [VERIFIED] (`mistral_raw.render`):
- tool answers go into the prompt as-is, inside `[TOOL_RESULTS]…[/TOOL_RESULTS]`;
- so the separator reaches the model as a literal tab character;
- the model's own earlier calls are re-rendered as JSON, where a tab appears as `\t`.

**Line 181 is shown next to indented lines** [VERIFIED]. For example, E seed 307, turn 16:

```
tests/conftest.py (lines 180-183 of 380)
180<TAB>    cookie_session_backend: ClientSideSessionBackend, mock_asgi_app: ASGIApp
181<TAB>) -> SessionMiddleware[ClientSideSessionBackend]:
182<TAB>    return SessionMiddleware(app=mock_asgi_app, backend=cookie_session_backend)
```

On turn 17, that run's next attempt introduced a leading tab.

**Could the separator be mistaken for indentation? [HYPOTHESIS]** Yes, for this line in particular:
- **For an indented line,** the separator is followed by spaces (`180<TAB>    cookie…`).
- **For line 181,** the text starts immediately after the tab (`181<TAB>) -> …`). Taking the line number off and
  keeping what follows it yields exactly `<TAB>) -> …`, the attempt observed.
- The separator was chosen so that it could not be mistaken for space indentation. It can still be kept as a tab.
- **Whether the model does this is not established.** Only the timing is (section 6).

## 5. Edit-tool refusal investigation

**The refusal for a whitespace-only mismatch** [VERIFIED] (`SharedTools._edit_file`, explicit mode):

```
error: the text to replace was not found in tests/conftest.py: it matches once apart from whitespace (indentation),
at line 181. These are the file's lines there (each is its number, a tab, then the text; copy the text exactly, with
its spaces):
181<TAB>) -> SessionMiddleware[ClientSideSessionBackend]:
```

| Question | Answer [VERIFIED] |
|---|---|
| Does it show the actual source line? | Yes: one line, as many as `old` has, in the numbered layout. |
| Does it reproduce the submitted text? | No. It shows only the file's line. |
| Does it say what differs? | No. It doesn't say that the submitted text has 4 spaces or a tab and the file's line has none. |
| Are tabs and spaces distinguishable in it? | In the characters, yes: the separator is a tab, and source indentation would be spaces after it. In the layout, they are adjacent: for line 181 the source text begins directly after the separator tab. |
| Is the wording ambiguous? | It says to "copy the text exactly, with its spaces". For a line with no spaces, that is accurate but gives no explicit cue that the correct leading whitespace is none [HYPOTHESIS that this matters]. |
| Is there enough information to rebuild the edit? | Yes, in principle: dropping `181<TAB>` gives the exact text. C's note gives the same text JSON-quoted (`old: ") -> SessionMiddleware[…]:"`), with no layout to remove. |

**The loop breaker** [VERIFIED]:
- from the third identical call on, the answer is "not answered again: you made this exact call at turns … Its answer
  is in your history";
- it does not repeat the error or the line.

**History compaction** [VERIFIED]: in the inspected windows (for example E seed 307, turns 14–23), compaction
superseded or summarised at most 1–2 earlier answers. So the error listings largely stayed in the model's context.
- [UNKNOWN] Which answer was superseded is not recorded.

**The response after a whitespace-only refusal:**
- The model's next line-181 attempt was most often the same call, or a second variant (four spaces, then a tab).
- In no case outside C did the next attempt drop the leading whitespace from a one-line `old`.

## 8. Root-cause assessment

**Established:**
- The line has no leading whitespace. The PRISM arms' one-line edits of it carried four spaces or a tab, and the edit
  tool correctly refused them.
- Without C's exact text, no one-line edit of the line was ever corrected. Every recovery outside C's notes came from
  a multi-line edit anchored on a correctly indented neighbour.
- Tabs entered only after the numbered `N<TAB>text` layout had shown line 181. The file has no tab, and PRISM showed
  the line unindented in every arm.
- The baseline avoided the problem: it never edited the line alone with whitespace.

**Not established:**
- **That the numbered layout causes the tab.** The timing is strongly consistent with it, but no counterfactual exists
  in these runs.
- **Which display is responsible:** the edit error, `read_file`/`prism_read`, or both.
- **Why the first attempt has four spaces:** copying from the neighbouring lines or the previous edit, or the model's
  own expectation of a continuation line's indentation.
- **Why the PRISM arms edit the line alone** while the baseline edits the block.
  - [HYPOTHESIS] The gateway's place-by-place worklist quotes line 181 alone. That may favour one-line edits, but this
    was not varied in the experiment.
- **Why, after a whitespace-only refusal that shows the exact line, the model repeats the refused call** rather than
  copying the line.

**Assessment:** the immediate cause is the model's mis-transcription of the leading whitespace of a single
unindented continuation line, followed by repetition. That much is verified. The origin of the tab is most plausibly
the display separator; that remains a hypothesis.

## 9. Potential future investigation directions (not implemented)

**Offline tests**, which need no new run:
1. Count, across all earlier phases' transcripts, how often a leading tab in `old` follows a numbered listing of that
   line, and how often a one-line edit of a line whose neighbours are indented carries their indentation.
2. Replay these runs' refused one-line `old` texts through the offline edit-fix rule (`phase3b_edit_replay.py` v2) to
   see how many line-181 refusals it would resolve. It strips leading whitespace and re-bases on the file's line, so
   it would cover both the four-space and the tab variants.

**Candidate changes**, each needing its own pre-registration:
- **Prevention:**
  - a listing separator that cannot be kept as whitespace, for example `181: ` or `181│`;
  - an error message that states the leading-whitespace difference ("your text starts with 1 tab; line 181 starts
    with none");
  - echoing the submitted text beside the file's line.
- **Recovery:**
  - the edit-fix rule above;
  - C's exact text;
  - a loop-breaker answer that repeats the file's exact line instead of "its answer is in your history".
- **Place presentation:** quoting a continuation line with its signature's first line, so a block edit is natural.

## 10. Limitations

| Limitation | Detail |
|---|---|
| Truncated answers | The turn logs keep 300 characters per tool answer. What the model saw beyond them is inferred from the tool code and the call's arguments. Examples: whether a `read_file` range included line 181 (from its header), and the error's numbered line (from the code and the "at line 181" marker). A displayed line cut off in the log was not reconstructed character by character. |

| Compaction | Which earlier answer it superseded or summarised in a turn is not recorded, only how many. |

| Post-hoc | Every interpretation was made after the outcomes were known, and is labelled. |
| Small numbers | 15 new tab variants and 4 never-recovered C runs: too few to compare arms on these measures. |
